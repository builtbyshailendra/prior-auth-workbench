# 9. First discovery workshop

A plan for the first two-hour session with the client. The aim isn't to present a solution. It's to learn how the work really happens, agree what success means, and leave with access to the things that will prove or disprove the hypotheses in this pack.

## Who's in the room

- VP Utilization Management (sponsor) for the first and last 20 minutes
- Intake supervisor, one intake coordinator, two nurse reviewers, one medical director
- An IT integration lead, and someone from compliance for the last 30 minutes

## Agenda

| Time | Topic | Output |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | Sponsor: why now, what a good pilot looks like | Draft success measures, agreed by the sponsor before they leave |
| 0:20 | Walk one real (de-identified) request from fax to decision, with the people who touch it | Corrected current-state map |
| 0:50 | Where does time go? Each role names their worst 15 minutes of a typical day | Ranked pain points |
| 1:10 | The checklists: what does a nurse look for, per pilot service? | First written checklist draft per service |
| 1:30 | Systems and access: what can a new tool read, and how? | Integration options and owners |
| 1:45 | Compliance: deadlines, denial reasons, AI policy | Constraints list, AI governance contact |
| 1:55 | Recap: decisions, open questions, who sends what by when | Action list |

## Questions to ask

**Sponsor**
- What would make you call this pilot a success in 90 days, and a failure?
- Which number does your boss ask you about?
- Who has to say yes before anything goes live?

**Intake**
- Show me the last request you re-keyed. What did you type, and from where?
- When you chase a missing document, how many calls does it usually take?
- How do you know a request is a duplicate today?

**Nurse reviewers**
- On this lumbar MRI case, which three facts decided it? Where in the packet were they?
- What do you wish was on the first page of every case?
- If software laid out those facts for you, what would you need to see to trust it?

**Medical director**
- What makes an escalated case easy or hard to decide?
- How do you write denial reasons today, and has one ever been overturned for its wording?

**IT**
- What's the approved way for a new system to read eligibility: API, replica or file?
- What would make you refuse this design?
- How are staff accounts and roles managed, and is there single sign-on?

**Compliance**
- Which events start, pause or stop the decision clock in your policies?
- Is there an AI-use policy covering PHI? Who approves AI vendors?

## Ask them to send

- 10 de-identified fax packets per pilot service
- Current checklists or policy documents for the 3 pilot services
- Last quarter's volumes by channel, and any deadline-miss reports
- 3 recent denial letters (de-identified)
- A system diagram, or names of the system owners

## Habits for the room

- **Let the people who do the work draw the map.** Correct it on the wall, not in a deck afterwards.
- **Write every number down as a hypothesis** until there's data behind it.
- **When operations and IT disagree, record both positions and the reason.** Don't settle it in the room.
- **End with named owners and dates,** or the next meeting repeats this one.
